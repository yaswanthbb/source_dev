RESEARCH SNAPSHOT · 05 OCT 2026 Architecture recommendation

From one-shot generation to an evidence engine.

The winning system is not a larger prompt. It is a persistent curriculum state, a multi-pass lesson factory, and an evaluation loop that learns from real student outcomes.

Core answer

Build all three—state, chained passes, and evals—but in that order. The “imaginary box” should be structured data, not an ever-growing chat transcript.

01 / DECISION

What makes it better than hand-prompting?

A strong general model can write one plausible lesson. A world-class course system must preserve intent over dozens of lessons, verify claims, test what students retain, and improve without silently regressing.

A fixed graph first. Controlled generation second. Measured learning third.

Leading documented architectures converge on a fixed curriculum, per-lesson multi-pass production, a quality layer, and a human gate. SkillFlow is the clearest engineering example: outline → draft → research/fact-check → critique → style → review. SkillFlow · live ↗

02 / TARGET ARCHITECTURE

Turn six isolated calls into one course compiler.

Keep AiGenerateService.complete() as the provider boundary. Replace each single-shot path with a staged workflow that reads and updates one roadmap-scoped source of truth.

00

Analyze

Inputs: learner profile, prior knowledge, outcome, constraints, teacher voice. Convert the course promise into measurable outcomes with Bloom verbs.

01

Curriculum graph

Generate modules, concepts, prerequisites, difficulty phases, and assessment coverage. Dedupe concepts before any prose is written.

02

Research brief

Retrieve from licensed, OER, or public-domain sources; extract claims and provenance. The draft sees the brief, not an unbounded scrape.

03

Lesson outline

Require the learning objective, concept sequence, worked example, likely misconception, prior-lesson callback, and next-lesson bridge.

04

Draft

Use the strongest suitable writing model. Feed only relevant graph context, terminology, teacher-persona rules, and compressed prerequisite recaps.

05

Fact-check

Check atomic claims against the research brief, surface unsupported statements, validate links, and run verbatim-overlap filtering.

06

Critique

Score accuracy, organization, relevance, adaptability, practicality, currency, challenge, and vividness with anchored evidence—not a single “quality” score. MDPI · index ↗

07

Revise + style

Repair only failed dimensions, then apply the roadmap’s voice. Keep “sound human” out of the factual drafting step so style cannot mask errors.

08

Validate + publish

Schema validation, callback integrity, Mermaid compile check, MCQ lint, source/license checks, rubric threshold, then a human gate.

Model routing belongs in the factory.Use inexpensive models for extraction, classification, and repair; reserve the strongest model for synthesis and judging. A role-based LLM factory with fallbacks is documented by syllabus-swarm. GitHub · index ↗
03 / THE “IMAGINARY BOX”

Memory should be queryable, inspectable, and hard to contradict.

Do not pour a million-token transcript into every call. Long-context models can still miss information in the middle. Persist structured state and hydrate only what the current lesson needs.

04 / LEARNING DESIGN

Optimize for durable learning, not fluent reading.

The course should feel simple at first, but the learning loop should deliberately require recall, variation, and transfer. In Dunlosky’s evidence review, practice testing and distributed practice were high-utility; elaborative interrogation, self-explanation, and interleaving were moderate-utility. Dunlosky et al. · index ↗

DESIGN RULE 01

Retrieve before rereading

End lessons with low-stakes recall and begin later lessons with a prerequisite retrieval hook. Do not mistake summaries for practice.

DESIGN RULE 02

Interleave for transfer

Mix current and earlier problem types in cumulative quizzes. The research brief reports a 43% delayed-test accuracy gain for interleaved versus blocked practice; this figure is index-derived and should be rechecked before external quotation.

DESIGN RULE 03

Fade the scaffolds

Move from fully worked example to completion problem to independent attempt. Difficulty should rise with demonstrated competence, not elapsed lessons.

05 / WHAT THE OUTCOME EVIDENCE SAYS

Guardrails decide whether AI helps or harms.

The strongest evidence in the brief is not about prose quality. It is about what learners can do later without the model.

−17%

Unrestricted help can create an illusion of learning.

A 2024 PNAS study of roughly 1,000 high-school students reported that unrestricted ChatGPT improved practice performance by 48% but reduced later unaided test scores by 17%. A guardrailed Socratic tutor improved practice by 127% and mitigated the harm.

0.63

Well-scaffolded tutoring can produce substantial gains.

A 2025 crossover RCT in Harvard introductory physics (194 students) reported an effect size of 0.63: median post-test 4.5 versus 3.5, in 49 versus 60 minutes. The tutor withheld full answers, required attempts, and used short feedback bursts.

Capability does not guarantee engagement.

In an 18-school Khanmigo evaluation, students reportedly used the tutor on about one-third of practice days and 17% of mistakes; it added no measured benefit over practice alone. The binding constraint appeared to be use, not access.

06 / MCQs & REVIEW

Questions should diagnose thinking, not just score answers.

Generated MCQs save time, but current evidence says they can be easier, lower-order, and somewhat more error-prone than human items. Treat generation as the first draft of an instrument.

Pre-publish lint

  • Stem contains the complete problem, phrased positively.
  • Options are homogeneous, independent, and similar in length.
  • No “all of the above”; use “none” sparingly.
  • Distractors come from real misconceptions.
  • Every option has a rationale.
  • Correct-answer positions are balanced.
  • Bloom target matches the objective.

MCQ checklist · index ↗

Post-answer psychometrics

  • Difficulty index by item and learner segment.
  • Discrimination index and point-biserial.
  • Distractor selection rate; flag dead distractors.
  • Guessing success rate and answer-position bias.
  • Misconception-family patterns for remediation.
  • Bloom distribution across each assessment.
  • Retire or regenerate unstable items.

PLOS review · index ↗

Upgrade review scheduling from fixed doubling to FSRS.FSRS models difficulty, stability, and retrievability and schedules to a target retention level. The brief reports about 22% greater precision than SM-2 and 15–20% fewer reviews for similar recall; those figures are index-derived. Add leech detection, lapse recovery, due-queue prioritization, and claim-targeted cards. ACM KDD · index ↗ FSRS tutorial · index ↗
07 / EVALUATION HARNESS

Quality needs four layers of proof.

Automated judges make iteration fast; only human calibration and actual learning outcomes make the score credible.

1Deterministic checks

Schema validity, broken links, unsupported callbacks, terminology drift, duplicate concepts, citation coverage, Mermaid compilation, MCQ lint.

2Anchored LLM rubrics

Per dimension: observable score anchor, evidence quote, failure explanation, proposed revision. Never ask only “is this good?”

3Human calibration

Experts independently score a stratified sample; calculate Cohen’s or Fleiss’ κ, refine the rubric, and repeat. “Judging the Judges” used this pattern with EQuIP. arXiv · index ↗

4Learning outcomes

Randomized A/B tests with a pre-registered delayed post-test. Assign variants deterministically by user ID; monitor engagement because availability is not usage.

Prompt operations are release engineering.Create immutable prompt versions, maintain a golden set per task, log promptVersion on every call, run same-input consistency tests, then deploy shadow → 10% → 25% → 50% → 100% with rollback. A source in the brief reports 30–40% less judge variance from anchored rubrics and a Cronbach’s α improvement from 0.393 to 0.798 after rubric refinement; those values are index-derived. Evaluation guide · index ↗
08 / IMPLEMENTATION ROADMAP

Ship the foundation before the spectacle.

The sequence below minimizes rework. Each phase has a hard gate so “more generation” never becomes a substitute for quality.

PHASE 1

Course bible + prompt ops

  • Add roadmap-scoped curriculum, term, concept, example, and provenance registries.
  • Version every prompt; store model, parameters, prompt version, inputs, outputs, latency, cost, and evaluation result.
  • Add per-task model defaults behind the existing provider abstraction.
  • Create golden sets for the six current generation paths.
Exit gate: a regeneration can reproduce its exact prompt/model context, and every lesson can resolve valid prerequisites.
PHASE 2

Multi-pass lesson and question factory

  • Outline → research → draft → fact-check → critique → revise/style.
  • Schema validation and repair at every boundary.
  • Callback validator, terminology checker, dedupe check, MCQ lint.
  • Human approve/edit/reject gate with reason codes.
Exit gate: no lesson publishes with unsupported claims, broken callbacks, schema failures, or critical rubric failures.
PHASE 3

Grounding, media, and fast evaluation

  • Curated lawful source ingestion and claim-level provenance.
  • Mermaid plan → generate → compile → critique → revise pipeline.
  • Anchored LLM judges calibrated to expert ratings.
  • Shadow prompt releases and percentage rollouts.
Exit gate: new prompt versions beat the baseline on every protected metric with no golden-set regression.
PHASE 4

Psychometrics and learning outcomes

  • FSRS with leech handling, claim-targeted cards, and due prioritization.
  • Item difficulty, discrimination, point-biserial, and distractor analytics.
  • Student-level A/B tests using delayed post-tests.
  • Feed outcome and item failures back into prompt and curriculum revisions.
Exit gate: a course version earns promotion because learners retain and transfer more—not because reviewers prefer its prose.

11 / OPEN QUESTIONS

What still needs direct validation.

These are research gaps, not footnotes to hide.

How much automation should each course allow?
The “Instructional Agents” paper describes four levels of human involvement, but detailed prompt appendices and full evaluation numbers were not directly re-verified. Recheck the paper before operationalizing its exact roles or quoting results. arXiv · index ↗
Can generated visual relevance be scored reliably?
The research found strong principles for removing decorative visuals, but no strong primary evidence for automatic relevance validation of generated lesson imagery. Start with human review and log visual failure modes.
Which model belongs at each stage?
Architecture sources support role-based routing, but no universal model winner exists here. Benchmark available NVIDIA NIM and Gemini models on your golden sets by stage, quality, latency, and total cost per approved lesson.
What is the right Bloom distribution?
There is evidence that AI items skew lower-order, but the ideal mix depends on domain, learner level, and objective. Define the distribution per module—not globally—and validate it against expert judgment and outcomes.
What does “better than human-made” mean?
Pre-register the claim. Suggested primary outcome: delayed unaided transfer test. Secondary outcomes: completion, time-on-task, confidence calibration, engagement, expert revision effort, and cost per mastered objective.
